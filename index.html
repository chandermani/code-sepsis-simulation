Code Sepsis

A branching urosepsis case for medical students. Your choices make the patient better or worse.

Arriving now

Margaret Ellis, 78, brought in by ambulance from her residential home. Staff say she's had 2 days of burning and frequency passing urine. Today she's drowsy and muddled (normally chatty and orientated), shivering, and holding her left side.

PMH: type 2 diabetes (metformin), hypertension (ramipril), CKD 3a (baseline creatinine 110). NKDA. Weight 60 kg. Exam: dry mucous membranes, cap refill 4 s, left renal angle tenderness. Glucose 14.2.

How to play
  • Your Stability Score starts at 5/10. Good decisions raise it; poor ones drop it.
  • If it hits 0, the patient arrests.
  • Delays cost time on the clock, and time matters in sepsis.
  • Teaching points are revealed in the debrief at the end.

Debrief
Core learning points
  • Spot sepsis early with NEWS2 and red flags. New confusion in an older adult isn't “just a UTI”.
  • Sepsis Six within the hour: oxygen, cultures, IV antibiotics, fluids, lactate, urine output.
  • Fluids in boluses with reassessment; vasopressors when fluid alone doesn't restore pressure.
  • Always look for a source to drain. An obstructed, infected kidney is an emergency.
  • Review the drug chart: sick-day rules and kidney-adjusted dosing.
  • Narrow antibiotics once cultures are back.
